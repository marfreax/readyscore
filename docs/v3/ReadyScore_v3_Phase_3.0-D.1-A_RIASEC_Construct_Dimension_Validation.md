# ReadyScore v3 — PHASE 3.0-D.1-A
# RIASEC Construct & Dimension Validation

**Product:** ReadyScore  
**Phase:** 3.0-D.1-A  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** VALIDATION GATE / DESIGN BASELINE  
**Parent:** 3.0-D.1 — RIASEC Measurement Specification  
**Purpose:** Validate the construct boundary and six-dimension architecture before creating the RIASEC item blueprint.

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This phase validates whether the proposed RIASEC model is sufficiently coherent to become the foundation for ReadyScore's first concrete interest instrument.

The current model is:

```text
R — Realistic
I — Investigative
A — Artistic
S — Social
E — Enterprising
C — Conventional
```

The objective is **not** yet to create questions.

The objective is to establish:

```text
WHAT RIASEC MEASURES
        ↓
WHAT EACH DIMENSION MEANS
        ↓
HOW THE DIMENSIONS DIFFER
        ↓
WHAT EVIDENCE BELONGS TO EACH DIMENSION
        ↓
WHAT EVIDENCE MUST NOT BE INCLUDED
```

Only after this gate is accepted should item construction begin.

---

# 2. VALIDATION BASIS

This document distinguishes three layers:

```text
Realistic
```

## B. ReadyScore product interpretation

ReadyScore needs to translate the framework into:

# 3. CORE CONSTRUCT

## 3.1 Construct Definition

For ReadyScore v3, RIASEC is defined as:

> A structured profile of an individual's relative vocational interests across six broad interest orientations: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional.

The central word is:

```text
RELATIVE
```

The purpose is to identify the pattern of interests, not to produce a universal measure of personal worth, intelligence, or capability.

---

# 4. CONSTRUCT BOUNDARY

RIASEC may provide evidence about:

```text
preferred activities
preferred work environments
preferred problem contexts
preferred interaction contexts
preferred forms of work
relative attraction to occupational environments
```

RIASEC does NOT directly measure:

```text
intelligence
aptitude
academic achievement
technical competence
personality as a whole
emotional intelligence
resilience
motivation
values
financial circumstances
actual career performance
guaranteed career success
```

This boundary must be preserved in product language.

---

# 5. MEASUREMENT UNIT

The measurement unit is:

```text
INTEREST
```

not:

```text
ABILITY
```

Therefore:

```text
"I enjoy analyzing complex problems."
```

is conceptually relevant.

But:

```text
"I am very good at mathematics."
```

is primarily an ability/self-perception statement.

A question bank must avoid systematically mixing these constructs.

---

# 6. SIX-DIMENSION MODEL

The validated product architecture is:

```text
RIASEC
│
├── R — Realistic
├── I — Investigative
├── A — Artistic
├── S — Social
├── E — Enterprising
└── C — Conventional
```

Each dimension represents a distinct interest orientation.

The scores should be preserved independently.

---

# 7. R — REALISTIC

## 7.1 Core Construct

Realistic represents interest toward:

```text
practical activity
hands-on work
objects/tools
machines
physical environments
building/fixing
operational activity
```

The emphasis is on attraction to **practical and tangible activity contexts**.

---

## 7.2 Positive Evidence

Potential evidence includes interest in:

```text
building something
repairing something
operating equipment
working with tools
working with physical materials
performing practical tasks
working outdoors or in physical environments
constructing or assembling
```

---

## 7.3 Negative Boundary

The following should not automatically be classified as Realistic:

```text
"I like engineering."
```

because "engineering" may contain:

```text
R + I
```

Likewise:

```text
"I like technology."
```

could indicate:

```text
R
I
```

depending on the activity context.

The item should describe the activity, not merely the profession label.

---

## 7.4 R Evidence Rule

Strong R evidence describes:

```text
WHAT THE PERSON LIKES TO DO
```

rather than:

```text
WHAT JOB TITLE SOUNDS ATTRACTIVE
```

---

# 8. I — INVESTIGATIVE

## 8.1 Core Construct

Investigative represents interest toward:

```text
investigation
analysis
reasoning
research
problem solving
understanding causes
exploring systems
discovering explanations
```

---

## 8.2 Positive Evidence

Potential evidence includes:

```text
finding out why something happens
analyzing information
solving complex problems
testing explanations
researching unfamiliar topics
examining patterns
understanding systems
```

---

## 8.3 Negative Boundary

Investigative must not become a proxy for:

```text
intelligence
IQ
academic achievement
mathematical ability
science grades
```

Example:

```text
"I enjoy figuring out how something works."
```

can be Investigative.

But:

```text
"I always get the highest math score."
```

is achievement evidence.

---

## 8.4 I Evidence Rule

Strong I evidence contains:

```text
curiosity
analysis
inquiry
reasoning
discovery
problem solving
```

without requiring the respondent to demonstrate actual ability.

---

# 13. DIMENSION DIFFERENTIATION

This is one of the most important gates.

The question bank must be able to distinguish dimensions that can appear superficially similar.

---

## 13.1 R vs I

### R

```text
"I like working directly with things."
```

### I

```text
"I like understanding why things work."
```

The distinction:

```text
R → practical interaction
I → investigation / explanation
```

A technology activity can contain both.

---

## 13.2 I vs A

### I

```text
"I enjoy finding the explanation."
```

### A

```text
"I enjoy creating a new expression or design."
```

The distinction:

```text
I → discovery / analysis
A → creation / expression
```

---

## 13.3 A vs S

### A

```text
"I enjoy expressing ideas creatively."
```

### S

```text
"I enjoy helping other people understand ideas."
```

The distinction:

```text
A → creative expression
S → human interaction / development
```

---

## 13.4 S vs E

### S

```text
"I enjoy helping someone develop."
```

### E

```text
"I enjoy persuading people to support an idea."
```

The distinction:

```text
S → helping / teaching
E → influence / leadership
```

---

## 13.5 E vs C

### E

```text
"I enjoy initiating and driving an activity."
```

### C

```text
"I enjoy organizing the process and keeping it structured."
```

The distinction:

```text
E → initiative / influence
C → structure / organization
```

---

## 13.6 C vs R

### C

```text
"I enjoy organizing information systematically."
```

### R

```text
"I enjoy working directly with equipment or physical materials."
```

The distinction:

```text
C → information/process structure
R → tangible/practical activity
```

---

# 14. DIMENSION OVERLAP IS ALLOWED

The model must not assume that dimensions are mutually exclusive.

A person can legitimately have:

```text
I + R
I + A
S + E
E + C
R + C
```

etc.

This is one reason the full six-dimensional vector must be preserved.

The goal is:

```text
PROFILE
```

not:

```text
ONE LABEL
```

---

# 15. PROFILE REPRESENTATION

Minimum representation:

```text
R score
I score
A score
S score
E score
C score
```

Then:

```text
rank 1
rank 2
rank 3
...
rank 6
```

Then optionally:

```text
topCode
```

The top code is a summary.

It must not replace the full profile.

---

# 16. TOP-CODE POLICY

ReadyScore may eventually display:

```text
RIA
IRC
SEC
...
```

as a concise profile code.

However:

```text
topCode
```

must be derived from the underlying six scores and stored/reconstructable.

The code must never become the only persisted result.

---

# 17. RELATIVE INTEREST PRINCIPLE

Consider:

```text
R 80
I 78
A 76
S 74
E 72
C 70
```

This indicates a broad profile.

Compare:

```text
R 92
I 74
A 58
S 49
E 44
C 37
```

This indicates stronger differentiation.

Therefore the interpretation layer should eventually distinguish:

```text
HIGH PROFILE DIFFERENTIATION
```

from:

```text
BROAD PROFILE
```

without inventing unsupported psychological meaning.

---

# 18. ITEM EVIDENCE MATRIX

Every item in the future blueprint should be mapped as:

```text
item
→ primary dimension
→ optional secondary analytical tag
→ construct evidence
```

Example:

```text
RIASEC-R-001
Primary: R
Evidence: hands-on activity
```

An item should have exactly one **primary scoring dimension** in the initial implementation.

This avoids ambiguous scoring.

---

Example:

```text
A
├── creativity

└── aesthetics
```

```text
S
├── helping
```

---

# 20. INDICATOR VALIDATION RULE

An indicator belongs to a dimension when:

```text
activity/context
    ↓
primarily expresses that interest orientation
```

It should not be assigned merely because the activity is commonly associated with a job.

Example:

```text
"Design a bridge."
```

could involve:

```text
R
I
A
```

depending on what the item is actually asking.

Therefore the question wording must make the intended interest signal clear.

---

# 21. OCCUPATION LABELS ARE NOT ITEMS

Avoid:

```text
"Saya tertarik menjadi dokter."
```

as the primary item design strategy.

Why?

Because the response can reflect:

```text
status
family expectation
income expectation
social prestige
familiarity
perceived job security
```

rather than pure interest.

Prefer activity/context items.

---

# 22. MAJOR LABELS ARE NOT PRIMARY ITEMS

Avoid:

```text
"Saya ingin kuliah Teknik Informatika."
```

as a direct measurement item.

The instrument should measure the underlying interest patterns.

Major mapping belongs downstream.

---

# 23. CAREER LABELS ARE DOWNSTREAM

The correct architecture is:

```text
Interest
    ↓
RIASEC Profile
    ↓
Interest Interpretation
    ↓
Study / Major Exploration
    ↓
Career Exploration
```

not:

```text
Question
    ↓
Career
```

---

# 24. CULTURAL LOCALIZATION

ReadyScore is intended for Indonesian users.

Items should therefore use familiar contexts.

Examples:

```text
sekolah
kampus
organisasi
proyek
kegiatan sehari-hari
hobi
teknologi
lingkungan kerja
```

However, localization must preserve the construct.

Do not change the underlying meaning merely to make an item sound local.

---

# 25. AGE APPROPRIATENESS

Because ReadyScore may be used by students, the question bank should be designed for the intended age range.

The production specification must eventually define:

```text
minimum age
maximum / target age
language complexity
context complexity
```

A question suitable for an adult professional may not be appropriate for a junior-high student.

---

# 26. RESPONSE BIAS

The design should consider:

```text
social desirability
prestige bias
family expectation
career-status bias
response acquiescence
central tendency
```

This is another reason to prefer activity-based items over prestigious occupation labels.

---

# 27. ITEM BALANCE

Initial target:

```text
R = equal representation
I = equal representation
A = equal representation
S = equal representation
E = equal representation
C = equal representation
```

This supports balanced measurement.

The final item count must be determined during the next phase.

---

# 28. REVERSE ITEMS

Reverse-keyed items should be used cautiously.

Potential issue:

```text
negative wording
```

can create comprehension difficulty, especially for younger respondents.

Therefore:

> Prefer clear positive-interest items unless reverse wording has a demonstrated measurement benefit.

---

# 29. RIASEC RESULT SHOULD NOT USE CURRENT V2 DOMAIN MODEL

The current v2 architecture has an eight-domain scoring structure.

That structure should **not** be reused for RIASEC merely for technical convenience.

RIASEC requires:

```text
R
I
A
S
E
C
```

as its own measurement dimensions.

This is an explicit v3 separation.

---

# 30. CURRENT SCORING ENGINE IMPLICATION

The current scoring engine:

```text
SCORING_V1
```

contains assumptions such as:

```text
DOMAIN_COUNT = 8
COMPLETE_DOMAIN_THRESHOLD = 6
LIKERT 1–5
0–100 normalization
domainScores
subdomainScores
indicatorScores
```

These assumptions are appropriate only for the current v2 instrument.

They must not be hard-coded as the universal v3 scoring engine.

RIASEC should eventually use:

```text
RIASEC_SCORE_V1
```

with:

```text
6 dimensions
RIASEC-specific sufficiency
RIASEC-specific interpretation
```

---

# 31. VALIDATED PRODUCT BOUNDARY

The product may say:

```text
"Memetakan pola minat."
```

It should not say:

```text
"Menentukan jurusan terbaik secara pasti."
```

The RIASEC result is:

```text
EVIDENCE
```

for future decision support.

It is not:

```text
DESTINY
```

---

# 32. CROSS-TEST ROLE

RIASEC's role in the future ReadyScore profile:

```text
RIASEC
→ INTEREST EVIDENCE
```

Potential complementary evidence:

```text
Cognitive
→ ABILITY EVIDENCE

Strength
→ RELATIVE STRENGTH EVIDENCE

DISC
→ BEHAVIORAL STYLE EVIDENCE

EQ/AQ
→ EMOTIONAL / ADAPTATION EVIDENCE
```

This is the foundation for a much stronger future recommendation engine.

---

# 33. RIASEC → STUDY DIRECTION

The future engine should answer:

```text
"Bidang studi apa yang layak saya eksplorasi?"
```

rather than:

```text
"Jurusan apa yang harus saya ambil?"
```

Potential output:

```text
High I + R
→ Explore technology / engineering / science

High S + A
→ Explore communication / education / creative-social areas

High E + S
→ Explore business / management / communication / people-oriented areas

High C + I
→ Explore information / finance / analytical structured areas
```

These are **illustrative mapping hypotheses**, not locked recommendation rules.

---

# 34. RIASEC → MAJOR FIT

The future Major Fit engine must combine:

```text
interest
+
ability
+
strength
+
context
```

Example:

```text
RIASEC = I/R
Cognitive = strong analytical
Strength = logical
        ↓
Technology / Engineering exploration
```

The actual major score must be defined in a later phase.

---

# 35. RIASEC → CAREER

The career layer should use occupational evidence:

```text
RIASEC profile
    ↓
occupation family
    ↓
role examples
    ↓
required capabilities
```

This allows the user to understand:

```text
interest fit
```

separately from:

```text
ability fit
```

# 36. VALIDATION QUESTIONS

Before item writing, the team should be able to answer:

### Construct

- [ ] Is RIASEC explicitly defined as vocational interest?
- [ ] Is interest clearly separated from ability?

### Dimensions

- [ ] Are R/I/A/S/E/C definitions clear?
- [ ] Can each dimension be distinguished from adjacent dimensions?

### Items

- [ ] Can an item be assigned to one primary dimension?
- [ ] Does the item measure interest rather than ability?
- [ ] Is the wording appropriate for the target age?

### Result

- [ ] Are all six dimensions preserved?
- [ ] Is the profile pattern preserved?
- [ ] Is topCode only a summary?

### Product

- [ ] Are study/major recommendations downstream?
- [ ] Are claims non-deterministic?
